How to Select the Next Word

Part 2: Training data, attention, and one word at a time

Learning Objective

  • Two ingredients go into every word an LLM produces: training data and your prompt
  • See how the model decides which ingredient matters most, word by word

Two Ingredients

  • Training data - millions of sentences the model has already read
  • Your prompt - the sentence you just handed it

The "Next Word" comes from a combination of these two sources

Attention

  • When predicting the next word, the model doesn't treat every word you typed equally
  • It decides which of your words matter most right now
  • That decision is called attention

Self-Attention

  • Every word in your prompt looks at every other word in your prompt
  • And asks: "how relevant are you to me?"
  • The words with the strongest pull get listened to the most

Let's Build a Tiny Model

  • Imagine our entire training data is just five sentences:
            The cat likes milk
            The dog likes bones
            The fish likes bubbles
            The cat hates water
            The dog hates cats

The Prompt

            The cat likes ___

What word comes next?

Step 1: Score Every Word

  • Model weights importance of each word
            Q(likes) . K(The)   = 0.02
            Q(likes) . K(cat)   = 0.58
            Q(likes) . K(likes) = 0.40
          
            The cat likes milk
            The dog likes bones
            The fish likes bubbles

Step 1: Score Every Word

"The" — 0.02

"cat" — 0.58

"likes" — 0.40

"cat" and "likes" together win almost all of the attention

Step 2: Attention Points

  • High attention on "cat" and "likes" means: both matter most for the next word
            The cat likes ___

But how does the model select the next word?

Training Phase

  • Training phase took place when model was created
  • Sees sentences featuring "cat"
  • Learns words associated with "cat"
  • Model develops associations like "cat" <-> "pet"

The cat ran like the wind

Meet the Actors

  • Imagine the model keeps one actor standing on stage for every word it knows
  • The milk actor only has 1 line: milk
  • When the spotlight shines on them, they say their line

Places, Everyone!

  • Training decided where they stand
  • Words with similar meanings are shoulder to shoulder
  • "milk," "water," "juice" - all clustered in the same corner of the stage

Attention Shines the Spotlight

  • Attention decided "cat" and "likes" both matter
  • Those two words point the spotlight to one part of the stage

The Spotlight is Crowded

  • Actors with similar meanings cluster close together
  • The spotlight doesn't land on one actor, it lands on a neighborhood
  • "milk" shouts loudest, but "water," "juice," and "soda" shout too

Sampling

  • Whoever spoke up becomes a candidate
  • The model rolls a weighted die to pick a word
  • "milk" was standing closest to the center. Wins most of the time

Step 3: Pick the Word

  • The spotlight lands near where "cat" and "likes" point together
  • A handful of actors shout, sampling picks one
            The cat likes milk

One Word at a Time

  • The model doesn't write a sentence
  • It writes one word, then re-reads everything - including its own new word - and does this whole process again

Brining it All Together

  • New prompt. Just one word:
            The ___

Choices, choices...

  • Any sentence starting with The is equally likely
  • Sampling selects a possibility at random

The Model's Short List

            cat    30%
            dog    25%
            fish   20%
            table  15%
            ...     10%

The Roll Lands On...

dog

Round Two: "The dog ___"

  • Now there are two words to attend over
  • Self-attention re-runs from scratch - "dog" pulls almost all of the weight
            The dog ___

A New Short List

            likes  55%
            hates  40%
            ...     5%

Sampling Again

likes

Round Three: "The dog likes ___"

            bones   98%
            ...      2%

Still a Roll - Just a Loaded One

  • Sampling still happens on this word
  • But at 98%, it almost always lands the same way
            The dog likes bones

The Motive

  • An LLM has exactly one motive: predict the next word
  • It decides which words matter using attention
  • Attention shines a spotlight on the stage that training already arranged
  • Sampling selects one of the candidate words
  • Repeat, one word at a time

The Takeaway

If you understand what pulls attention, you can steer what gets predicted.

That's prompt engineering.
